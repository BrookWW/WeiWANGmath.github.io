Beyond minimizers: stationary harmonic maps and computable regularity constants

From Evans's sphere theorem to general targets: the Rivière–Struwe proof in Lean, and exact rational bounds for the small-energy threshold and Hölder estimate.

After formalizing small-energy regularity for energy-minimizing harmonic maps, the next question is natural: how much of the conclusion survives if we retain the first variations but give up the minimizing inequality? This leads to stationary harmonic maps, where the geometry still produces a monotonicity formula, but the comparison argument from the minimizing case is no longer available.

The previous post followed a proof built around harmonic replacement, projection, and admissible competitors. The stationary project instead uses an antisymmetric system, a Coulomb frame, and compensated estimates. It also makes the small-energy threshold and the Hölder coefficient computable from certified geometric data.

The mathematical story has three parts: how small-energy regularity passed from spheres to general targets, which proof route the formalization follows, and how the constants in that route become exact rational numbers.

The result, at a glance

For every source dimension n≥2n\geq2n≥2, a stationary weakly harmonic map into a compact embedded C2C^2C2 target has a unique C0,1/2C^{0,1/2}C0,1/2 representative on the inner half ball when its normalized energy is sufficiently small. For a smooth target, the same representative is smooth.

Given a certified extension of the normal projection and a rational derivative bound, the energy threshold and Hölder coefficient are explicit rational-valued functions. Their values are conservative admissible bounds.

From minimizing maps to stationary maps

Let N⊂RqN\subset\mathbb R^qN⊂Rq be a nonempty compact embedded submanifold without boundary, and let Ω⊂Rn\Omega\subset\mathbb R^nΩ⊂Rn be open. We keep the energy convention of the minimizing project:

E(u;B)=∫B∣Du∣HS2 dx,Θu(a,r)=r2−nE(u;Br(a)).\begin{gathered} E(u;B)=\int_B|Du|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,dx,\\ \Theta_u(a,r)=r^{2-n}E(u;B_r(a)). \end{gathered}E(u;B)=∫B​∣Du∣HS2​dx,Θu​(a,r)=r2−nE(u;Br​(a)).​

There is no factor of one half. The displayed weak gradient DuDuDu is the same object in Sobolev membership, the variational identities, and the energy.

Weak harmonicity is the vanishing of the first variation under compactly supported tangent variations of the map. Domain stationarity is a further identity: for every X∈Cc∞(Ω,Rn)X\in C_c^\infty(\Omega,\mathbb R^n)X∈Cc∞​(Ω,Rn),

∫Ω∣Du∣2div⁡X dx=2∫Ω∑i,j=1n⟨Diu,Dju⟩ ∂iXj dx.\begin{gathered} \int_\Omega|Du|^2\operatorname{div}X\,dx\\ =2\int_\Omega\sum_{i,j=1}^n \langle D_i u,D_j u\rangle\,\partial_iX_j\,dx. \end{gathered}∫Ω​∣Du∣2divXdx=2∫Ω​i,j=1∑n​⟨Di​u,Dj​u⟩∂i​Xj​dx.​

In this post, a stationary harmonic map satisfies both conditions. An energy minimizer supplies them, but neither first-variation identity supplies the inequality against arbitrary competitors that a minimizing proof uses. At weak regularity, domain stationarity must also be stated explicitly; it is not silently inferred from weak harmonicity.

The distinction changes the proof. Monotonicity survives because its input is the domain-stationarity identity. The energy comparison with a projected harmonic replacement requires minimality. To recover decay for stationary maps, the argument must use more of the structure of the weak equation.

How the theory reached general targets

The classical background begins with Schoen and Uhlenbeck's 1982 regularity theory for energy-minimizing harmonic maps. Passing to stationary maps required a different argument, since their energy need not decrease under finite variations.

A key special case was the sphere. In 1991, Lawrence C. Evans proved partial regularity for stationary harmonic maps into spheres. His proof combined the sphere's algebraic structure with compensated estimates and Hardy–BMO duality. The exceptional set has zero (n−2)(n-2)(n−2)-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

In 1993, Fabrice Béthuel extended this stationary regularity theory to general compact Riemannian targets. Thus the general-target theorem predates the later antisymmetric-system formulation. The issue for formalization is which proof of that theorem to implement.

Work Setting Place in the argument
Schoen–Uhlenbeck, 1982 Energy-minimizing maps The minimizing regularity theory underlying the previous project
Evans, 1991 Stationary maps into spheres Uses the sphere's special structure and compensation
Béthuel, 1993 Stationary maps into general compact targets Extends the stationary theory beyond spheres
Rivière–Struwe, 2008 Antisymmetric systems with critical Morrey control Supplies the gauge method chosen for this formalization

Tristan Rivière's antisymmetric formulation reorganized two-dimensional conformally invariant variational equations. Rivière and Michael Struwe then developed the higher-dimensional method in Partial regularity for harmonic maps and related problems, published in 2008. Its Theorem 2.1 treats the stationary small-energy problem for closed C2C^2C2 targets in dimensions at least three. The preprint appeared in 2006.

Their method combines a skew-symmetric coefficient, a Coulomb gauge, and critical Morrey estimates. It avoids constructing a frame adapted to the target's tangent bundle and makes the C2C^2C2 geometric assumption explicit. The present project follows this route, including dimension two in its public theorem.

The theorem selected for Lean

Fix n≥2n\geq2n≥2 and a compact embedded C2C^2C2 target N⊂RqN\subset\mathbb R^qN⊂Rq, with its induced Euclidean metric. Suppose

and suppose uuu is weakly harmonic under tangent map variations and domain stationary. The formal theorem supplies ε∗>0\varepsilon_*>0ε∗​>0 and C≥0C\geq0C≥0, depending only on n,Nn,Nn,N, with the following conclusion.

If

then there is a unique continuous representative www on BR/2(a)B_{R/2}(a)BR/2​(a). It agrees with uuu almost everywhere, takes values in NNN at every point, and satisfies

The exponent is fixed at α=1/2\alpha=1/2α=1/2. The constants are chosen before the domain, map, weak gradient, center, and radius. The statement includes zero energy and covers the entire inner half ball. Uniqueness is among all continuous ambient-valued representatives agreeing almost everywhere with uuu there.

For a compact smooth target, a second theorem proves that this same www is C∞C^\inftyC∞, retaining the quantitative Hölder bound. Its public statement does not include quantitative estimates for every derivative order. The C2C^2C2 theorem and the smooth-target corollary therefore have distinct conclusions.

The proof route: geometry, gauge, compensation

Let Q(y)Q(y)Q(y) be the orthogonal projection onto the normal space (TyN)⊥(T_yN)^\perp(Ty​N)⊥. The embedded C2C^2C2 geometry makes QQQ a C1C^1C1 field along NNN. Choose a compactly supported ambient C1C^1C1 extension GGG and a bound ℓ\ellℓ for its derivative.

Tangency of the weak gradient and the original tangent variations give an antisymmetric equation:

−Δu=∑i=1nAi Diu,-\Delta u=\sum_{i=1}^n A_i\,D_i u,−Δu=i=1∑n​Ai​Di​u,

where

Ai=Q(u) ∂i(Q∘u)−∂i(Q∘u) Q(u),AiT=−Ai.\begin{gathered} A_i=Q(u)\,\partial_i(Q\circ u) -\partial_i(Q\circ u)\,Q(u),\\ A_i^T=-A_i. \end{gathered}Ai​=Q(u)∂i​(Q∘u)−∂i​(Q∘u)Q(u),AiT​=−Ai​.​

This commutator also gives

∣A∣HS≤2q ℓ ∣Du∣HS.|A|_{\mathrm{HS}}\leq2\sqrt q\,\ell\,|Du|_{\mathrm{HS}}.∣A∣HS​≤2q​ℓ∣Du∣HS​.

The skew symmetry is the structure that the compensation argument exploits. An arbitrary quadratic right-hand side would not carry this information.

Independently, domain stationarity gives the exact monotonicity identity:

Θu(a,s)−Θu(a,r)=2∫Bs(a)∖Br(a)∣x−a∣2−n∣∂rad,au∣2 dx≥0.\begin{gathered} \Theta_u(a,s)-\Theta_u(a,r)\\ =2\int_{B_s(a)\setminus B_r(a)} |x-a|^{2-n}|\partial_{\mathrm{rad},a}u|^2\,dx \geq0. \end{gathered}Θu​(a,s)−Θu​(a,r)=2∫Bs​(a)∖Br​(a)​∣x−a∣2−n∣∂rad,a​u∣2dx≥0.​

Monotonicity at nearby centers converts small energy on the original ball into control on all smaller balls. To state that control precisely, write

M2(F;D)2=sup⁡x∈Dt>0t2−n∫Bt(x)∩D∣F∣2 dx.\mathcal M_2(F;D)^2 =\sup_{\substack{x\in D\\t>0}} t^{2-n}\int_{B_t(x)\cap D}|F|^2\,dx.M2​(F;D)2=x∈Dt>0​sup​t2−n∫Bt​(x)∩D​∣F∣2dx.

M2(Du;D)2+M2(A;D)2≤MΘu(a,R),\mathcal M_2(Du;D)^2+\mathcal M_2(A;D)^2 \leq M\Theta_u(a,R),M2​(Du;D)2+M2​(A;D)2≤MΘu​(a,R),

with the explicit geometric factor

M=(1+4qℓ2)4n−2.M=(1+4q\ell^2)4^{n-2}.M=(1+4qℓ2)4n−2.

This is the link between stationarity and the gauge construction. A single integral on one ball becomes critical Morrey control of both the original gradient and its connection.

For sufficiently small connection, the proof constructs P∈W1,2(D,SO(q))P\in W^{1,2}(D,\mathrm{SO}(q))P∈W1,2(D,SO(q)) such that

Zi=PT∂iP+PTAiPZ_i=P^T\partial_iP+P^TA_iPZi​=PT∂i​P+PTAi​P

is divergence-free with its natural zero normal flux. Its Morrey bound controls DPDPDP and this actual transformed connection together. No orientation of NNN or global normal frame is required, and the main theorem does not ask the caller to supply a gauge.

The next step applies a Hodge decomposition to a genuine cutoff vvv of the original map minus its mean. The exact part, coexact part, and harmonic remainder are retained through their estimates. Div–curl compensation controls the difficult pairings, and an all-center envelope iteration yields improved gradient growth. With p=1+1/(2n)p=1+1/(2n)p=1+1/(2n), its bound has the form

∫Br(x)∣Dv∣p dx≤Cpowηprn−p/2,\int_{B_r(x)}|Dv|^p\,dx \leq C_{\mathrm{pow}}\eta^p r^{n-p/2},∫Br​(x)​∣Dv∣pdx≤Cpow​ηprn−p/2,

where η\etaη controls the joint Morrey amplitude. This is an LpL^pLp growth estimate for the cutoff, from which the Morrey argument recovers exponent 1/21/21/2 for the original map.

Compactness of the actual target supplies the bounded cutoff needed in these pairings. Accordingly, the project proves the compact-target harmonic-map theorem; it does not export a general regularity theorem for arbitrary, possibly unbounded Euclidean-valued antisymmetric systems.

Ball averages then recover the continuous representative, its almost-everywhere agreement with uuu, its pointwise target values, and its uniqueness. Applying the intermediate two-thirds-ball estimate on B3R/4(a)B_{3R/4}(a)B3R/4​(a) reaches exactly BR/2(a)B_{R/2}(a)BR/2​(a).

For a smooth target, the same representative satisfies a quadratic Poisson equation with smooth geometric coefficients. The reused analytic infrastructure proves a classical first derivative and then applies Poisson Schauder estimates and a smooth bootstrap. This is where the stationary development reconnects with the earlier minimizing project.

What the geometric certificate contains

An abstract existence theorem says that its constants depend only on n,Nn,Nn,N. Numerical evaluation needs a finite geometric input. Here that input is a certified pair (G,ℓ)(G,\ell)(G,ℓ):

G∈Cc1(Rq,L(Rq,Rq)),ℓ∈Q≥0,G\in C_c^1\bigl(\mathbb R^q,\mathcal L(\mathbb R^q,\mathbb R^q)\bigr), \qquad \ell\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0},G∈Cc1​(Rq,L(Rq,Rq)),ℓ∈Q≥0​,
G(y)=Q(y)(y∈N),∥DG(y)∥op≤ℓ(y∈Rq).\begin{gathered} G(y)=Q(y)\quad(y\in N),\\ \|DG(y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\ell \quad(y\in\mathbb R^q). \end{gathered}G(y)=Q(y)(y∈N),∥DG(y)∥op​≤ℓ(y∈Rq).​

The derivative bound must hold throughout the ambient extension, including its cutoff region. A bound only along NNN does not supply this certificate.

The norm convention matters: DGDGDG maps Euclidean vectors to matrices equipped with their operator norm. Gradient and connection energies use the Hilbert–Schmidt norm. The factor 4qℓ24q\ell^24qℓ2 above records the corresponding estimate.

The formal development proves that every target in the theorem admits suitable data. It also proves an effective theorem when those data are supplied. It does not assert an algorithm extracting a numerical certificate from an arbitrary abstract manifold. Once the certificate is available, evaluating the constants requires no computation of the map or its Coulomb frame.

This gives a stronger numerical interface than the constant extraction discussed in the previous post: the effective theorem itself uses the explicit rational-valued functions. Positivity and admissibility are proved for those functions, and the original existential target-only theorem remains available.
